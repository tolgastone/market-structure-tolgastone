# v1.3.0 — Cement Completion

Released 2026-11-15.

## Added

The new **Impulse completion** setting provides three policies:

- **Single Cement** (default): the existing behavior. An UP impulse completes on bearish Cement; a DOWN impulse completes on bullish Cement.
- **Two Cements**: complete on a second opposite Cement. Intervening bars are allowed; the two events need not be consecutive.
- **First Cement Boundary**: the first opposite Cement creates a candidate. A later close below its wick low completes an UP impulse; a later close above its wick high completes a DOWN impulse. The later candle need not itself be Cement.

## Candidate rules

A strictly new impulse high/low clears the candidate and count. That new-extreme bar can seed a fresh candidate if it qualifies as opposite Cement. Equal extremes do not reset. There is no timeout; boundary confirmation requires a later chart bar and a strict close beyond the candidate boundary.

Structural break transitions also clear candidate state. Cement lookback remains independent of the number of confirmation events. Initial direction still uses the existing Cement bootstrap, and accepted opposite-boundary interruptions retain priority under the selected structural break source/tolerance.

## Display and timing

The Cement markers switch shows the selected policy's completion marker. In First Cement Boundary mode the marked confirmation candle need not itself be Cement; candidate bars receive no extra marker.

All policies retain realtime evaluation. Open-bar candidates and results remain provisional, and completed endpoints are drawn at their tracked extreme bars. No fixed pivot window or closed-bar-only guard is added.

## Compatibility

Single Cement remains the default and retains the preceding structural behavior. The alternative policies can change completion time, extremes and the later swing chain, so displayed Fibonacci, OTE and FVG ranges may follow different swings. Their calculation formulas and FVG lifecycle rules are unchanged.

No fractal mode is included. No completion policy is claimed to be universally more accurate.
